We treat rewatching as a guilty pleasure, as if a work's value expired the moment its plot was known. But the first view is the worst view. You were busy learning what happens. You missed everything else.

## Plot is the shallowest layer

A [slow-burn crime series](/tv/slow-burn-crime/) or an [antihero arc](/tv/antihero-golden/) reveals its real structure only once you know the ending and can watch the denial form. The second pass is, frankly, a better show.

## Rewatching is curation you already did

When you return to a [slow cinema film](/film/slow-cinema-patience/) or a [narrative walking game](/games/narrative-walking/), you are re-entering a feeling you already mapped. That is efficient discovery — no algorithm required, no risk of a bad recommendation. You know this one works for you.

## Make it intentional

Keep a short "rewatch" shelf. One [british indie record](/music/british-indie-90s/) you return to every winter. One film for bad days. The library you own matters more than the one you scroll.

Rewatching isn't repetition. It's the view that finally sees the film.
