Genre tags are useful and misleading in equal measure. "Drama" describes a thousand unrelated nights. "RPG" spans cozy life-sims and punishing [soulslike lineages](/games/soulslike-lineage/). The tag is the address; the mood is the person inside.

## Tags describe structure, not temperature

A [metroidvania](/games/metroidvania-map/) and a cozy farming sim are both "games," but one asks for tension and the other for safety. The genre tells you the rules; it does not tell you how the evening feels.

## How we translate

Our editorial filter reads the tag as a hint, then checks the work against the list's thesis. A film tagged "drama" lands in [slow cinema](/film/slow-cinema-patience/) only if it argues for patience, not just because the label matches. The tag gets you to the door; the feeling decides if you stay.

## A practical habit

Next time you browse, swap the question. Instead of "what genre?" ask "what does this ask of my attention?" A [limited series](/tv/limited-series-tight/) asks for a weekend; a [lofi record](/music/late-night-focus-lofi/) asks for nothing. That distinction is the whole game.

Tags get you started. Mood is where you land.
