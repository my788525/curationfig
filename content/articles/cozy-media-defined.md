Curation falls apart the moment words stop meaning anything. "Cozy," "atmospheric,"
"cyberpunk" — these get attached to everything and therefore describe nothing. This
guide defines the tones we actually use on CurationFig, so a label means a feeling
you can count on, not a marketing tag. Think of it as the vocabulary for
[mood-based curation](/articles/how-mood-shapes-media-choice/).

## Cozy

**Cozy** means low stakes and permission to stop. A cozy work asks nothing of you:
no fail state, no timer breathing down your neck, no plot that punishes looking
away. It is media as rest. A [cozy game](/games/cozy-games/) is the purest form —
you water plants and the game thanks you — but the feeling also lives in a gentle
[rainy-night film](/music/rainy-window/) or a series you have seen so often it is
furniture. If a work makes you feel *safe to be still*, it is cozy. If it makes
you feel you should be doing better, it is not.

## Atmospheric

**Atmospheric** means the texture carries more than the story. Light, reverb,
weather, the length of a take — these do the emotional work, and the plot is almost
incidental. Atmospheric media rewards attention to *how it feels* over *what
happens*. Our [slow cinema](/film/slow-cinema-patience/) and [neo-noir](/film/neo-noir-shadows/)
lists are atmospheric by design. A work can be atmospheric and tense, or
atmospheric and calm — the word describes the *method*, not the mood.

## Cyberpunk

**Cyberpunk** is a tone before it is a genre: neon, decay, the individual against
the system, technology that has outrun its makers. It is almost always
atmospheric, frequently dark, and usually carries a specific kind of lonely energy —
crowds everywhere, connection nowhere. You will find it in films, in certain
[game lineages](/games/soulslike-lineage/), and in synth-heavy records. The
telling sign: the future looks amazing and feels terrible.

## Melancholy vs. Sad

We separate these on purpose. **Sad** is a drop — a loss, a cry. **Melancholy** is
a weather — a quiet, survivable ache that can actually be pleasant to sit in. Most
of our [lonely-night](/music/lonely-tuesday/) and [bittersweet-ending](/music/bittersweet-endings/)
lists are melancholy, not sad, because the point is the company the feeling keeps,
not a wound. If a work leaves you full rather than empty, that is melancholy.

## Tense vs. Scary

**Tense** is anticipation — the string held, the door not yet opened. **Scary** is
the payoff. We curate a lot of tension and comparatively little scare, because
tension is a mood you can choose on a Tuesday, while a real scare is a commitment.
Our [midnight dread](/music/midnight-dread/) list is mostly tension by design.

## Why definitions matter for discovery

When a label means a feeling, a list built on it actually helps you. When a label
is just a genre, you get a pile. The whole site is an argument for the first
approach: define the tone honestly, then gather the works that deliver it across
every medium, and let people find their night by how they want to feel. That is
also why our [cross-medium experiments](/articles/mixing-media/) work — the tone
translates, the genre does not.

## A starting vocabulary

If you want to self-curate, start with five tones: cozy, atmospheric, melancholy,
tense, warm. Almost every evening you have ever planned well was one of those, or a
blend. Name it, then go find it — in a film, a game, or a record.
